## 5. File the Annual Report every fiscal year

- [ ] Record the ACM fiscal year: **July 1 through June 30**.
- [ ] Assign a report owner (typically the officer responsible for administration/finances) and a reviewer early in the fiscal year.
- [ ] Set internal deadlines well before the ACM submission deadline; verify the actual deadline in the current Administrative Interface.
- [ ] Before starting, confirm at least one current officer is listed in the Chapter Officers section.
- [ ] Log in to the Chapter Administrative Interface and select **Annual Reports**.
- [ ] Complete the mandatory report sections, following the interface's sequence and prompts:
  - [ ] **Basic Finances & Census** — complete this first; gather current chapter census and financial figures.
  - [ ] **Cash & Disbursements** — reconcile balances, receipts, payments, and supporting records.
  - [ ] **Meetings & Recent Activities** — prepare an accurate list of meetings and completed activities for the reporting period.
  - [ ] **Upcoming Activities** — describe planned events and activities with dates or expected timing where available.
  - [ ] **Authorization & Signatures** — obtain the required officer authorization/signatures.
  - [ ] **Finalization** — review all fields, resolve validation issues, submit, and save the confirmation/receipt.
- [ ] For a **professional chapter**, also complete the applicable sections listed in the excerpt:
  - [ ] Income to Third Parties.
  - [ ] Income from Donors.
  - [ ] Any other professional-chapter sections shown in the current report.
- [ ] For a **U.S. chapter only**, complete the applicable IRS Form 990 information/requirements. Do not apply this item to a non-U.S. chapter unless ACM directs otherwise.
- [ ] Save a copy of the submitted report and supporting financial/activity records in the chapter's restricted records location.
- [ ] Verify the report status shows submitted/complete in the interface; do not assume saving a draft counts as filing.
- [ ] Put the next fiscal year's report cycle on the chapter calendar.

## 6. Prevent probationary status

- [ ] Track Annual Report submission status every year and follow up before the deadline.
- [ ] If a report is missing or the chapter is marked **Probationary**, contact the ACM Local Activities Coordinator at [local_activities@acm.org](mailto:local_activities@acm.org) promptly for guidance.
- [ ] Submit the overdue current Annual Report as soon as possible; the excerpt says submitting it while on probation changes the chapter status to **Active**.
- [ ] Check whether the chapter's public listing has been removed while probationary and correct public descriptions if needed.
- [ ] If the chapter is a SIG or ACM-W chapter, coordinate with the person overseeing activities for that SIG or ACM-W.
- [ ] If an ACM Regional Council covers the chapter's area, follow up with the council as appropriate.

## 7. Protect continuity and avoid de-chartering

- [ ] Keep an annual compliance calendar for officer updates, member roster maintenance, activities, finances, and report preparation.
- [ ] At each officer handover, transfer records for finances, activities, members, account access, and prior reports.
- [ ] If the chapter has been probationary and misses Annual Reports for **two consecutive years**, treat de-chartering as an urgent risk and contact ACM Local Activities for help.
- [ ] If already de-chartered, check eligibility immediately: the excerpt says the chapter must have been de-chartered **less than two years** to seek reactivation through a Viability Report.

- [ ] Identify the officer submitting the report and provide the requested information about them.
- [ ] Complete the general chapter information requested.
- [ ] Submit the report and be prepared to provide additional information if ACM requests it.
- [ ] Track ACM's review decision; reactivation is determined by ACM and is not automatic.
- [ ] If the chapter is a SIG or ACM-W chapter, coordinate with the relevant overseeing individual; if covered by a Regional Council, coordinate as appropriate.

## 9. Suggested recurring schedule

- [ ] **At the start of each fiscal year (July):** assign the report owner/reviewer, verify account access and officer records, and open a finance/activity tracking file.
- [ ] **After every leadership change:** update the official officer list and transfer Chapter web account access and records.

- [ ] **Then:** curate real articles, event recaps, and project examples from the archive with permissions and attribution.
- [ ] **Finally:** add dated chapter statistics only when verified, and consider optional features such as live campus widgets only if they serve a clear visitor need.

# Award-Submission-Inspired Website Checklist

ACM's Outstanding Chapter Website prompt asks chapters to explain the website's **design and content, noteworthy features, how it was created, how it is kept up to date, and provide its URL**. The older submissions repeatedly describe mobile-friendly navigation, current news, event histories, team/contact information, newcomer guidance, photos, registration, and a clear update/maintenance process. Examples include [ABES ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/abes-acm-student-chapter-website.pdf), [UCR ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/acm-student-chapter-at-ucr-website.pdf), [IIT (ISM) Dhanbad ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/iit-ism-dhanbad-acm-student-chapter-website.pdf), [JUIT ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/juit-acm-student-chapter-website.pdf), [McMaster ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/mcmaster-university-acm-student-chapter-website.pdf), and [WCE ACM](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/chapters/student-chapter-excellence-awards-submissions-2017/outstanding-chapter-website-submissions-2017/wce-acm-student-chapter-website.pdf). The linked 2017 rubric spreadsheet was not machine-readable in this review, so the checklist below reflects the published prompt and accessible submission essays, not a transcription of every scoring cell.

## 29. Make the site's design easy to use

- [ ] Manually preview the complete site at common phone, tablet, and desktop sizes and confirm navigation and key tasks remain straightforward. Code inspection alone does not confirm rendered behavior.

## 30. Make announcements and event history useful

- [ ] Publish real, chapter-approved announcements with dates in reverse chronological order. The current API has events and blog posts, but no announcements feed; do not treat demo seed records as public announcements.
- [x] Added a chronological upcoming/ongoing event list and a reverse-chronological Past Events archive, with a direct jump link between them.
- [x] Added a “Next up” homepage feature for the earliest listed event, using live event data and a direct link to event details; intra-club events retain their visibility label.
- [ ] Add organizer/contact, dedicated registration, recap, and resource fields to event records and maintain them; the current event model only provides a general `link` plus basic schedule/location/description/sub-event fields.
- [ ] Publish results or winners for competitions only when verified and approved for public sharing.
- [x] Kept event information one click from the homepage through the event feed and direct Events links in the primary navigation and footer.
- [ ] Add useful follow-up content: slides, recordings, notes, photos, or project resources when permission and access allow.
- [ ] Make registration and feedback forms mobile-friendly, accessible, and clear about collected data and its use when the chapter provides or links approved forms. The current app has no event registration or feedback form; external event links open in a new tab.

## 31. Help newcomers decide to join and take the next step

- [ ] Add a newcomer FAQ covering how to join, who may participate, membership requirements, recruitment timing, expected commitment, and where events are held; answer only what the chapter has confirmed.
- [ ] Provide a working signup or interest form when recruitment is open; show a clear closed/recruitment-not-open state at other times.
- [ ] Explain the difference between ACM membership, chapter participation, and any member-only activities accurately.
- [ ] State where the chapter meets or how to find the clubroom, if there is a stable public location.
- [ ] Provide a monitored contact route for questions and set a realistic response expectation; don't promise a response time the team cannot maintain.
- [ ] Add concise “Why join?” and “What we do” content connected to actual benefits, active groups, and current programs.

## 32. Put faces and safe contact routes to chapter roles

- [ ] Publish a current officer/team page with role names and responsibilities so students know whom to approach.
- [ ] Include faculty sponsor/advisor information where approved and current.
- [ ] Use role-based emails or a chapter contact form as the default; publish personal emails or profile links only with consent and a maintenance plan.
- [ ] Add member directories only if there is a clear chapter need, access controls, consent, and data-retention plan. Do not copy old submissions that exposed membership numbers, membership dates, student IDs, or broad member contact details.
- [ ] Include an alumni contact/community path only if it is actively maintained and alumni have consented to the chosen visibility.

## 33. Use photos and media to show real chapter activity

- [ ] Curate an event gallery that shows the range of real chapter activities, with dates, event names, and useful captions or alt text.
- [ ] Obtain permission for identifiable people in photos and confirm rights to use images, posters, recordings, and submitted work.
- [ ] Optimize images for mobile and provide accessible text alternatives; avoid galleries that block the rest of the page while loading.
- [ ] Link gallery items to event recaps or related projects where helpful.
- [ ] Assign an owner to remove outdated, duplicate, or unapproved material.

## 34. Add interaction only when the chapter can support it

- [ ] Assess whether event RSVPs, feedback forms, member profiles, discussion threads, or resource downloads solve a current chapter problem before building them.
- [ ] For signups or accounts, collect only necessary information, explain its use, restrict access, define retention/deletion, and secure the service.
- [ ] For comments, discussions, or anonymous feedback, define moderation, safety/escalation, spam handling, and a monitored response owner before launch.
- [ ] For attendance tracking, avoid publishing sensitive attendance or membership data; obtain consent and use a justified, secure method.
- [ ] For paid events, confirm payment, refund, recordkeeping, and approval responsibilities before integrating a payment provider.
- [ ] Use integrations that are active and maintained; adapt the useful idea (RSVP/discussion/follow-up) without depending on a defunct social platform.

## 35. Show how the site is built and maintained

- [ ] Document the current stack, hosting, deployment steps, content sources, and recovery/rollback procedure in maintainer documentation.
- [ ] Identify a webmaster or web team and a faculty/leadership escalation contact; assign backups so knowledge survives officer turnover.
- [ ] Define who can publish or edit events, articles, project pages, team details, and contact information.
- [ ] Keep a lightweight content update routine: after events, publish recaps/results; after elections, update the team; each term, review links and pages.
- [ ] Record how contributions are reviewed and deployed, including repository access handover and dependency/security maintenance.
- [ ] Keep demo/sample data clearly separate from live chapter content and review production content before each release.
- [ ] Track basic site health (availability, broken links, form delivery, and error reports) without collecting unnecessary visitor data.
- [ ] Keep the public website URL and backup contact information current in chapter records and award applications.

## 36. Prepare an award-ready website description

- [ ] Maintain a short, factual website overview describing audience, purpose, design choices, and content organization.
- [ ] Keep a list of noteworthy features that are live and demonstrable (for example, Yantra pages, event archive, project showcase, blog, registration, or accessibility features).
- [ ] Document who created the current site, what technologies/hosting it uses, and what work was contributed by chapter members.
- [ ] Explain the content update workflow and how the site is handed over to future officers.
- [ ] Save the canonical public URL and confirm it works on phone and desktop without login.
- [ ] Collect verifiable evidence (screenshots, analytics summaries with privacy-safe aggregation, update history, working links, and content examples) before submitting an award application.
- [ ] Avoid unsupported visitor/member statistics, obsolete feature claims, or screenshots that expose personal data.
